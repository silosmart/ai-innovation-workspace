# Reading the Gap, Assessment

A unit in critical AI literacy. M.A. in Artificial Intelligence Innovation.

This is the assessment for the unit. There is nothing new to produce for it. You already made everything you need while doing the activity across the three lessons. Here you go back over that work, write down the gaps you noticed, and judge how sure you can be about each one. That judging is what the whole unit teaches, and it is what gets graded.

---

## What you learn to do here

By the end of this unit you can:

1. Find the places where an AI's reading of your own images parts from what you meant by them.
2. Tell apart what you can point to, what you are assuming, and what you cannot explain, using your own work as the evidence.
3. Read your own use of an AI tool through the four relations from the lectures, including where your relation to the tool shifted.
4. Work out, from your own experience, how your way of reading has changed and where the tool would slip out of sight for you later.

---

## How the unit's pieces feed this assessment

Each lesson produces something, and this assessment reads across all three. So the assessment is the summative piece for the whole unit, not for any single lesson.

- **Lesson 1 produced** `my-interpretation.md`, your own meaning written before any AI read your screenshots.
- **Lesson 2 produced** your running list of the gaps you noticed across the model, the second agent, and the image generator.
- **Lesson 3 produced** `final-prompt.md` and the coach's readback of your prompt record.

Each lesson also has a short completion checkpoint, listed at the end of this document, so the work is captured as you go rather than piled up at the end.

## What you submit

Three things. You will already have the first two from the activity.

**1. Your prompt record.** The file called `prompts.md`, which your coding agent saved on its own during the activity, in order. You do not edit it, you submit it as is. If you worked in a browser chat tool instead and kept your own prompt record by hand, submit that, it serves the same purpose.

**2. Your saved files.** `my-interpretation.md`, `screenshot-reading.md`, `peer-reading.md`, your generated image, your two before-and-after screenshots of the site (the site saves nothing, so you take these yourself, one before your edit and one after), and `final-prompt.md`.

**3. Your reflection.** A new document you write, by following the steps below.

## How to write the reflection, step by step

You will end up with a short document. First you list the gaps you noticed, and for each one you say how sure you can be about it, where it shows, and which relation you were in when you noticed it. Then you name which relation barely showed up across the session. Then you turn the lens on yourself, working out your own embodiment ahead and reflecting on how your reading has already changed. This is your own self-assessment of your work, so the judgments are yours throughout.

A fill-in worksheet for these steps is in the repo as [assessment-student-worksheet.txt](assessment-student-worksheet.txt).

### Step 1, open your prompt record and your files
Open `prompts.md` and read back through your own prompts, top to bottom. Keep your saved files nearby, because you will point to them.

### Step 2, write down the gaps you noticed
A gap is one place where what a tool gave you parted from what you meant by your own screenshots. Write at least five gaps, across at least three different tools. Each gap is one or two plain sentences.

### Step 3, for each gap, point to where it shows
Next to each gap, name the exact place a person can go to and see it, a specific prompt in `prompts.md`, or a specific spot in a file or your image. If you cannot find a place that shows it, that is not a failure, it is information, and Step 4 is where that goes.

### Step 4, label each gap one of three ways
- **I can point to it.** You named a real spot, and anyone looking would see the same thing.
- **I'm assuming it.** You believe it, but you cannot point to a spot that settles it.
- **I can't explain it.** You noticed it but cannot say why, and you leave it open rather than inventing a reason.

### Step 5, for each gap, name the relation you were in
Using the four relations from the lectures, name which one you were in at the moment you noticed each gap. Hermeneutic, you read the tool's output like a text to interpret. Alterity, you treated it as another party you were in dialogue with. Background, it shaped things underneath while you barely registered it. Embodiment, you looked through it without noticing it was there. Where your relation shifted from one to another during a gap, for example reading the output as a text, then pushing back so it became another party, name the shift and what made it happen.

### Step 6, name the relation that barely showed up
Across the whole session, name the one relation that barely showed up for you. For most people that is embodiment, because looking through a tool without noticing it comes from long, repeated use, and one sitting rarely gets there. Whatever was true for you, say which relations were present and which barely showed.

### Step 7, the reflexive turn
Two parts, looking forward and looking back.

Looking forward, work out your own embodiment ahead of time, grounded in three concrete things. The task, the specific repeated use, for example captioning every photo for your church's archive. The tipping point, what would make you stop checking, for example once it is right often enough that opening the originals to compare feels like wasted time. The loss, the specific thing you would stop noticing, for example that it quietly drops the older people at the back of a photo.

Looking back, reflect on how doing this has already changed your reading, pinned to two concrete things. One specific thing you will now notice in your own material that you would have walked past before. One specific way your reading changed. This is the reflexive part, you are reading your own way of reading, not just the tools.

### Step 8, write it up as prose
The steps above are your scaffold, the worksheet where you gather and sort. Your submitted reflection is a piece of writing, about 600 to 1000 words, that works through your gaps, the relations you were in, and your reflexive turn in your own voice and in full sentences, not a filled-in form. Set aside about two to three hours for the writing, since you already made the files during the activity. A finished example is in [assessment-worked-example.txt](assessment-worked-example.txt).

## How it is graded

There is no correct reading of your archive to arrive at, so the grade is not about a right answer. It is about how honestly and accurately you sorted what you noticed, how well you read your own work through the relations, and how concretely you turned the lens on yourself. Five criteria, each judged against what is in your reflection and your prompt record. The grader marks the level whose description best fits, then brings the five to an overall.

| Criterion | Strong | Adequate | Developing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pointing holds up** | Every gap marked "I can point to it" names a specific prompt in `prompts.md` or a specific spot in a file, and that spot, when checked, shows what you said. | Most do. One or two point to a spot that does not clearly show the thing. | Several name no spot, or point to a spot that does not show what you said. |
| **Guesses kept separate** | Everything you cannot tie to a spot is marked "I'm assuming," not "I can point to it." | One or two guesses are marked "I can point to it," but most sorting is right. | Guesses are repeatedly marked "I can point to it," so beliefs stand in for shown things. |
| **The unexplained left open** | At least one gap is marked "I can't explain it," names a real thing, and is left open rather than closed with a made-up reason. | An "I can't explain it" is present but thin, or a guess is offered alongside it. | Nothing is marked "I can't explain it," or unknowns are filled with invented reasons. |
| **Reading the gaps through the relations** | Names the relation you were in at each gap, names where it shifted and what made it shift, and names which relation barely showed up across the session. | Names relations at most gaps and the one that barely showed up, but misses the shifts or names them loosely. | Misnames the relations, or treats them as a checklist not tied to the gaps. |
| **The reflexive turn** | Works out your embodiment ahead with a task, a tipping point, and a loss, and the meta-reflection names one thing you will now notice in your own material that you would have walked past, and one way your reading changed. | The embodiment or the meta-reflection is present but general, missing one of the concrete parts. | The reflexive turn is vague or absent, or stays at "I learned a lot." |

The "I can't explain it" kind counts as much as the "I can point to it" kind, because naming what you cannot account for is the opposite of what these tools do when they hand you a confident answer.

**Bringing the five together.** The overall follows the best fit. Strong overall means mostly Strong with none at Developing. Adequate overall means mostly Adequate with no more than one at Developing. Developing overall means two or more at Developing. The letter or number in the gradebook follows your program's scale for that level.

**Seeing this ahead of time.** You get this rubric before you write, because you cannot judge your own work against a standard kept from you, and judging your own work honestly is the thing being assessed.

The grader's version, with a box to mark and space for notes, is in [assessment-grader-worksheet.txt](assessment-grader-worksheet.txt).

## Grading structure for the unit

| Component | Weight | How graded |
|---|---|---|
| Reflection (summative) | 70% | Five-criterion rubric, by best fit |
| Three lesson checkpoints, 5% each | 15% | Completion, the saved file plus a short knowledge check |
| Gallery participation | 15% | Completion, post one gap and respond to two |
| Optional live session | up to 5% extra | Participation, optional, recorded, with an asynchronous path for the same credit |

Most of the grade sits on one honest summative piece, the rest on completion of the lighter work, which is the standard shape for a self-paced adult course in a short term.

## Lesson checkpoints and knowledge checks

Each lesson ends with a completion checkpoint, the lesson's saved file plus a short knowledge check. The knowledge checks are native quizzes in the course platform, placed after each lesson video, auto-graded for completion, no upload. In production these become in-video checks. The questions:

**Lesson 1, Breakdown.** Submit `my-interpretation.md`.
1. Why do you write down what each screenshot means to you and save it before any AI reads them?
2. Match each relation to its one-line description: hermeneutic, alterity, background, embodiment.
3. What does the coaching agent you build carry into the work?

**Lesson 2, Inquiry.** Submit your running list of gaps.
1. In this unit, what is the gap?
2. When the model names a type it cannot point to on the screen, what does that show you?
3. A gap between your meaning and the tool's output is best understood as a reading from a position you can learn from, or as a tool error. Which?

**Lesson 3, Transformation.** Submit `final-prompt.md` and the coach's readback.
1. What is the point of the coach reading your prompt record back to you?
2. Which relation usually barely shows up in a single session, and why?
3. The goal of the unit is to reach the correct reading of your archive. True or false?

## Feedback and revision

Feedback returns on the rubric with a short comment naming where your sorting held and where it slipped. One revision may be offered within the term, following program policy, so a Developing on a criterion is a place to strengthen rather than a closed door.

---

## Academic integrity and AI use

Using the tools is the assignment, so working with AI throughout is expected. You may ask an AI to confirm one thing only, whether a spot you named exists in your files, which is a check on your own pointing, not a judgment made for you. The sorting, the relations, and the reflection are your own writing. A gap that points to a prompt which is not in your record does not hold up, so honest pointing is the straightforward path. Integrity here comes from your own evidence, not from surveillance.

---

## Technology and access

Everything here is plain text and image files. You submit through the course platform, the files or your whole workspace folder. Because the work is just files, it opens on any device, works with a screen reader, and needs no special software.

For a large group, collecting the files and confirming a named spot exists in the record can be automated, while the judgment of what a spot shows and the level for each criterion stays with the grader, who can overturn anything the automation flags. The machine finds and confirms, the person sees and decides.

---

## Why it is built this way

You do not produce anything extra for the grade, your prompt record saved itself while you worked, so the evidence is a byproduct of doing the activity. The reflection then asks the one thing the unit is about, tell what you can point to from what you are guessing, name what you cannot explain, read your own relations to the tool, and turn the lens on yourself. So the thing you are graded on and the thing you were taught are the same thing.
