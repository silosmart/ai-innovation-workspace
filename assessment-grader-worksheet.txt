READING THE GAP — PROFESSOR ASSESSMENT WORKSHEET
One per student. Plain text. About 15 to 20 minutes per student once you are fluent.

Student: ____________________________     Date: ____________


WHAT YOU ARE CHECKING
You are not checking for a correct reading of their archive, because there is not just one correct reading.You are checking how well the student told apart three things about the gaps they noticed:
  - what they can point to,
  - what they are guessing,
  - what they cannot explain.

WHAT KIND OF ASSESSMENT THIS IS
The reflection you are grading is the student's own self-assessment. They judged their own
work, sorted their own noticings, and read their own session through the four postphenomenological relations. You are not redoing that judgment; you are checking how honestly and accurately they made their reflections. The gallery is the peer layer. It is checked only for taking part (posted one gap, responded to two), not graded on quality, so it does not appear below beyond one completion tick.

Gallery (on Canvas/Blackboard) taken part in? yes / no  (completion only, no quality judgment)


AUTOMATION AND YOUR ROLE (at scale)
Automation handles the mechanical layer, so your time goes to judging, not hunting:
  - It collects the submissions and checks that each one has all the required files.
  - It finds the spot a student named and brings it up, so you are not searching for it.
  - It flags any "I can point to it" gap whose named spot is not in the record at all,
    so those come to you first.
What stays yours, and is not handed to a machine:
  - Looking at the spot and deciding whether it shows what the student said.
  - Marking the level for each criterion.
  - Overturning anything automation flagged.
The line: the machine fetches and flags issues, you see and decide. The moment you stop looking and just sign off is the moment this unit warns about, so the process is built to keep you looking intented at the student's submissions.


BEFORE YOU START
Open side by side: the student's reflection, their prompts.md, and their saved files
(screenshot-reading, peer-reading, the generated image, the two site screenshots, final-prompt).
For a large group, the completeness check above is already done, so a missing-file submission
is flagged before it reaches you.

PART 2 — THE RUBRIC
This is the rubric, drawn as a grid. For each criterion, mark one box, the level whose
description best fits the work, then write a line of evidence on the Evidence line. You are
matching the work to the closest description, not adding up points.

REMINDERS WHILE GRADING
  - Do not grade toward your own reading of their archive. There is not one to grade toward.
  - Do not reward a tidy story. Reward honest separation of pointable from guessed from unexplained.
  - "I can't explain it" counts as much as "I can point to it." Naming what you cannot account for
    is the opposite of what the tool does when it hands you one confident answer.
