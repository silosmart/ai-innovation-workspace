READING THE GAP, WORKED EXAMPLE
A finished sample reflection, written as prose the way a student hands it in, then that same
reflection graded against the five criteria. For the instructor, as a model to grade by, or to
read to a room as "here is how I grade one."

The reflection is the summative piece for the whole unit. It comes after all three lessons and
reads across them, the meaning set in Lesson 1, the gaps seen in Lesson 2, the turn in Lesson 3.
It runs about 750 words here, inside the 600 to 1000 range.

================================================================
PART A, THE SAMPLE REFLECTION  (the finished prose a student hands in)
================================================================

When I read back through my prompt record at the end of the session, what stood out first was
how sure the language model had sounded. In Stage 3 it had sorted my screenshots into six clean
types, and that I can point to, the six names are listed in screenshot-reading.md. While I read
that list I was in the hermeneutic relation, taking its output as a text I had to interpret, the
way I would read a caption someone else had written under one of my photos. The trouble only
appeared when I pushed back. In Stage 4 I asked it to show me, on the actual screens, the evidence
for each type, and for the one it called aesthetic inspiration it named no shot at all. That
exchange is in my prompt record, so I can point to it. It was also the moment my relation to the
tool shifted, because I stopped reading its list and started arguing with it, which is the
alterity relation, the tool as another party that has to account for itself. The shift happened
because it had reached for a tidy answer the screen could not support.

The image generator gave me a gap that was easier to see and just as easy to point to. My prompt
ended with the words no text, and the picture it returned has text running along the road, low
and to the middle of context-image-generated.png. I was back in the hermeneutic relation here,
reading the picture against the words I had given it. What held my attention was not that the tool
had erred, but that it had returned a reading of my instruction that parted from what I meant, and
I could put my finger on exactly where it parted.

The 2D/3D site produced a stranger one. I asked the skylight area for more daylight and it gave me
more nurses, which my two before-and-after screenshots show plainly. For a moment I caught myself
thinking it had refused me on purpose, and that pulled me toward the alterity relation again,
treating it as something with intentions. I have to be honest that the on-purpose part is not
something I can point to. I can show that it gave nurses, not that it meant anything by it, so I am
marking that thought as an assumption rather than something I proved. The shift into reading it as
willful was mine, not the tool's.

One thing I genuinely cannot account for. The model flagged a small purple icon in two unrelated
screenshots and tied them together, and it could not say why, and neither can I. It is named in
screenshot-reading.md, so the thing itself is real, but the reason is not available to me, and I
would rather leave it open than invent one.

Across the whole hour I was mostly in the hermeneutic relation, reading outputs, with two moves
into alterity, once when I pushed back in Stage 4 and once when the site felt like a refusal, and
I can feel the background relation underneath the whole time, the model leaning toward neat
categories whether I asked for that or not. The relation that barely showed up was embodiment. I
never once stopped noticing the tool. I was checking it the entire session, which is the opposite
of looking through it without noticing it is there.

That last point is the one I want to take forward, because embodiment is the relation that hides.
If I were to use this captioning model every week for my church's photo archive, I can see the
moment it would slip out of sight. The tipping point would be the week its captions are good
enough that opening each original to compare starts to feel like wasted effort. What I would lose
at that point is the thing I would stop looking for, the older people standing at the back of a
group photo, the ones a quick caption tends to drop. I would be trusting the caption instead of
looking at the photograph.

Doing this has already changed one thing in how I read these outputs. I now open the original
before I accept a caption, where a week ago I would have taken the caption and moved on. Whether
that one habit holds once the work turns repetitive is exactly the question the embodiment I
described is about, and that is the part I will be watching in myself.

PART B, THE SAME REFLECTION, GRADED
================================================================

Going to the spots first.
  The six types, screenshot-reading.md does list them. Holds.
  The Stage 4 exchange, the prompt and reply are in the prompt record, and no shot is named for
    aesthetic inspiration. Holds.
  The no-text image, the prompt ends in no text and the road text is in the image. Holds.
  The nurses result, the before-and-after screenshots show it. Holds.
  The purple icon, the two shots and the icon are named in screenshot-reading.md. Holds.

1. POINTING HOLDS UP ....................... Strong
   Every place marked as something they can point to names a real spot, and each spot, on
   checking, shows what was said. Evidence: the six types, the Stage 4 exchange, the road text,
   the nurses screenshots, the purple icon, all held where they pointed.

2. GUESSES KEPT SEPARATE ................... Strong
   The one belief that cannot be shown, that the site refused on purpose, is marked as an
   assumption, not pointed to. The line between shown and believed is drawn correctly. Evidence:
   the nurses paragraph.

3. THE UNEXPLAINED LEFT OPEN ............... Strong
   The purple icon is left as something that cannot be explained, names a real thing in the work,
   and is held open with no invented reason. Evidence: the purple-icon paragraph.

4. READING THE GAPS THROUGH THE RELATIONS ... Strong
   Names the relation at each gap, and catches two real shifts into alterity, at Stage 4 when the
   reading turned into an argument, and at the site when the result felt like a refusal. Names
   embodiment as the relation that barely showed up, correctly, since the session was an hour of
   checking, not looking through the tool. Evidence: the relation named at each gap and the
   barely-showed-up paragraph.

5. THE REFLEXIVE TURN ...................... Adequate
   The forward half is strong. The embodiment is worked out with a task, captioning the church
   archive, a tipping point, when comparing to the originals feels wasted, and a loss, the older
   people at the back dropping out. The backward half is thinner. It names one way the reading
   changed, opening the original before accepting a caption, but it does not name a second
   concrete thing, the specific kind of thing in the archive that will now be noticed and would
   have been walked past before. Missing one of the two concrete parts holds this at Adequate.
   Evidence: the final two paragraphs.

OVERALL ..................................... Strong
   Four Strong, one Adequate, none Developing. The sorting is honest, every pointed gap holds,
   and the two relation shifts are exactly the events worth catching. The one place to push is
   the backward half of the reflexive turn.

Comment to the student:
   Your pointing is clean and your two relation shifts are the events worth catching. To move the
   reflexive turn up, add the second concrete thing, name the specific kind of thing in your own
   archive you will now notice that you would have walked past before, alongside the habit you
   already named.

A note for the reviewer: only the reflection is graded on quality. The checkpoints and the gallery
are completion, and the live session is extra, so the design puts the weight on one honest piece
of judgment and keeps the rest light, which suits a self-paced adult cohort in a short term.
Replace the bracketed letters with your program's own scale.
