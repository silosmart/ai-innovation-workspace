## Unit synopsis

This unit teaches one idea, the gap between what you mean by your own images and what an AI makes of them. There is no single correct reading of an image, only readings from a position. When you hand your own screenshots to an AI, it returns a reading from its position, and the distance between that and what you meant is the gap. The unit treats the gap as the thing to learn from rather than an error to fix, because noticing where a tool's reading parts from your intention is information about the tool and about yourself.

The unit moves in three lessons. You set your own meaning first, then you watch the gap open across several tools, then you turn the lens on yourself. No coding is assumed, you work through plain prompts and your own phone screenshots, and the technical layer stays under the hood while you work at the level of meaning.

- **Lesson 1, Breakdown.** You build a small coaching agent in your own voice and write down what your screenshots mean to you before any AI reads them. The point is the first encounter where the tool resists or surprises you, and you catch yourself treating it as another party, which is where it stops being invisible.
- **Lesson 2, Inquiry.** You run your archive through a model, a second agent, and an image generator, and you read where each tool's output parts from what you meant. This is reflection while still inside the doing, the gap seen across several tools rather than one.
- **Lesson 3, Transformation.** You bring your meaning and the tools' readings into one master prompt, have the coach read your whole session back, and carry the gap-reading into a real use in your own life or work.

### Where this unit sits

## What you will be able to do

By the end of this unit you can:

1. Treat an AI's reading of your own material as one reading from a position, not the truth, and keep your own meaning central rather than handing judgment to the tool.
2. On any of your own material, find where a tool's output parts from what you meant, and tell apart what you can point to, what you are assuming, and what you cannot explain.
3. Read your own use of a tool through the four relations, including where your relation shifted, and watch how you came to read the way you do.
4. Carry the gap-reading into a real use you choose, so the skill changes a practice rather than staying an insight.

## Lesson 1, Breakdown

**Activity stages 1 and 2.** Checkpoint, submit `my-interpretation.md` plus a short knowledge check on the four relations. Completion.

**Video synopsis, about five minutes.** The video opens on the problem, that we let tools read our images before we have said what we meant, so we cannot tell afterward what the tool changed. It introduces the four relations as the unit's language. Then it shows this lesson's move, building a small coaching agent in your own voice and writing what each screenshot means to you, saved as `my-interpretation.md` before any AI touches it. It closes by naming that file as the fixed record of your own meaning, the thing the rest of the unit measures the gap against.

## Lesson 2, Inquiry

**Activity stages 3 through 6. This is the recorded video.** Checkpoint, submit your running list of gaps plus a knowledge check placed after the video. Completion.

**Video synopsis, about five to ten minutes.** This video does two jobs at once, which is why it sits in the middle and reaches both ways.

Looking back, it reviews Lesson 1, the coaching agent you built and the language of the four relations, so a returning student is reminded of the stance they set and of the saved meaning they will now read the gap against.

Looking forward, it sets up the rest of the unit. It shows the gap opening across several tools, the model's confident sort of your archive, a type it names but cannot point to on the screen, a second agent splitting your shots by a different logic, and an image that adds or drops things you never asked for. Then it names gap-reading as the single move the whole unit turns on, used in Lesson 1 to set your meaning, here to see the gap open, and in Lesson 3 to read yourself. So the video justifies its placement, it is both the look back and the hinge forward.

## Assessment and grading

The assessment is a separate brief, in [ASSESSMENT.md](ASSESSMENT.md). It consumes the activity's outputs, so nothing is produced just for the grade. The graded piece is one summative reflection at the end of the unit, which reads across all three lessons.

| Component | Weight | How graded |
|---|---|---|
| Reflection (summative) | 70% | Five-criterion rubric, by best fit |
| Three lesson checkpoints, 5% each | 15% | Completion, saved file plus knowledge check |
| Gallery participation | 15% | Completion, post one gap and respond to two |
| Optional live session, instructor-hosted guess-the-intention over video | up to 5% extra | Participation, optional, recorded, asynchronous path available |

The overall rubric level maps to the program's standard letter scale. The full rubric is in the assessment document and the student worksheet, shown before students write.

## Technology, access, and integrity

- **Technology.** Plain text and image files throughout. Everything opens on any device, works with a screen reader, and needs no special software. Submission is through the course platform.
- **Academic integrity and AI use.** Using the tools is the assignment, so working with AI is expected. The sorting, the relations, and the reflection are the student's own writing. A gap that points to a prompt not in the student's own record does not hold up, so integrity comes from their own evidence, not from surveillance.

---

## A note on the design approach

For anyone reading the architecture, the unit is built by working backward from the end. The transformed practice is the goal, the reflection is the evidence that it happened, and the lessons build to it, which is the Understanding by Design logic of Wiggins and McTighe. The same arc, seen as the student's experience, runs breakdown, inquiry, transformation, a reflective sequence drawn from the instructor's own doctoral work, grounded in Baumer, King and Kitchener, and Kant's reflective judgment. The two are the same arc from two angles, the planning logic and the lived one, and both end at the same transformed student.
